## Quantum-inequality check (new notebook Section 3, td-a2a4cf) + spec corrections

### Summary
Added a section the spec lacked: the Ford–Roman quantum inequality applied to the cosine wall. The author's form |ρ|·τ⁴ ≤ const is correct (const = 3ħ/(32π²c³) = 3.7 × 10⁻⁶² J·s⁴/m³). A 1 m wall exceeds it by ~10⁶⁴ (α = 0.1, v = c, worst case in the design's favour). The overshoot grows as Δ², so thick walls make it worse. Owner approved adding the section and said the spec (written by Claude iOS) may be improved; "Vorwaller Metric" was restored as the metric's name.

### What Was Done
- `warp/curvature.py`: symbolic (sympy) Riemann and Einstein tensors for ds² = −dt² + (dx − S dt)² + dy² + dz² with any S(t,x,y,z), in the Eulerian orthonormal frame, lambdified on (S, ∂S, ∂∂S); a chain rule for steady bubbles. Verified three ways: against Alcubierre eq. 19 (1e-17), against P&F eq. 18, and against an independent explicit-metric derivation of all 21 components.
- `warp/quantum.py`: QI constant, r_min from the max frame Riemann component (global worst case, or along the observer's path), Lorentzian sampling along the real geodesic, violation factor, P&F eq. 22.
- `shapes.alcubierre_ddf`, `cosine_wall_ddf`. Tests: 85 pass.
- Notebook Section 3 + `figures/03_quantum_inequality.png`.

### Key Technical Insights
- **The energy density has no time-derivative terms.** G_0̂0̂ = −(S_y² + S_z²)/4 for *any* S(t,x,y,z) (flat slices, unit lapse). Spec Task 5's premise ("time-derivative terms add to the stress-energy") is wrong for the energy density. Time dependence enters the stresses (G_îĵ ∋ S_tx), the curvature (and so the QI window) and the geodesics. Spec Task 5 and the cs.md rule were rewritten; my Section 2 sentence was corrected.
- The largest frame curvature of the cosine wall is R_t̂x̂t̂x̂ = β²f″ at the outer edge on the axis (for β ≥ 0.5): exactly (8/3)× P&F's eq. 18 component. Along the observer's own path it is ~1.32×. The headline uses the global max, which is ~7× looser than P&F; the path-only value is ~4× tighter than the headline.
- QI "passes" only for Δ ≲ 5 × 10⁻³³ m ≈ 307 L_P (α = 0.1), at ~10⁻⁴² s, where neither QFT in curved space nor GR is trustworthy.

## Task 3: dual-shell design (notebook Section 4) + external physics review (Gemini, AGY2)

### Summary
Section 4 tests the author's dual-shell formula exactly as written, plus three alternatives: both shells inward and 180° phase (from the spec), and a squared drive (cos², sin²), which is our suggestion and not in the notes. As written the ship goes nowhere: it rocks within ~19 cm each 1 ns cycle and the average speed is 0. The formula's 90° (cos/sin) is the always-on choice; the sketch's 180° switches both shells off together. With two shells, steady motion and steady energy are incompatible. The cheapest steady split (45% / 55% over the two walls) needs 3.35 × 10²⁹ kg, half of a single wall: the thick-wall idea again. Three shells *could* hand off continuously with both speed and energy constant.
