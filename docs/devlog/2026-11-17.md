## Shape-function module + Task 1 (wall shapes)

### Summary
Built `warp/shapes.py` (tested) and notebook Section 1 comparing Alcubierre's tanh wall with the author's cosine wall at Δ = 1, 10, 100 and 1000 m.

### What Was Done

- Notebook Section 1 → `figures/01_wall_shapes.png`, `figures/01b_one_metre_walls.png`.

### Key Technical Insight
- The project owner is not a physicist and will only give input on project structure. Physics must be self-verified against the papers and with numerical cross-checks.
- Thickness yardstick: P&F define a tanh wall's thickness as 1/(max slope), Δ ≈ 2/σ, so a 1 m wall is σ = 2/m. That matches the author's σ = 2.0 in the dual-shell design. By the same yardstick the cosine Δ = 1 m wall is 2/π ≈ 0.64 m.
- The author's π/(2Δ) max-slope formula is exactly right (checked numerically).
- **Heads-up for Task 2:** the author's cosine wall grows *outward* (R → R+Δ), and P&F's total energy is E = −(v²/12)∫r²(f′)²dr. Once Δ ≫ R the wall sits at r ~ Δ/2, so E may stop falling and start *rising* with Δ. Compute it; don't assume.
- **Also for Task 2:** the spec's T⁰⁰ has `v_s²/4`, which needs v_s in units of c (β²) to be dimensionally right in SI. My quick arithmetic of P&F eq. 28 at R = 100 m, Δ = 1 m gives ~0.56 M☉ against their stated "about a quarter of a solar mass". Check this numerically.

### Preview finding for Task 2 (don't overclaim before then)
A quick check of ∫r²(f′)²dr for the cosine wall at R = 100 m gives:

| Δ (m) | cost (m) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 12,461 |
| 10 | 1,361 |
| 100 | 282 |
| 300 | 269 |
| 1000 | 484 |
| 3000 | 1,174 |

The cost **bottoms out near Δ ≈ 200 m, then rises**, because the outward-growing wall sits at ever larger r. Thickening is the right lever up to about the bubble radius, but not without limit. Section 2 must show this honestly. The Section 1 captions were softened so they don't promise "thicker is always better".

## Task 2: total energy vs wall thickness (notebook Section 2)

### Summary
