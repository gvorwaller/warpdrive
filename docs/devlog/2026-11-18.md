# 2026-11-18 Devlog

## Task 6 (phase 2): WarpFactory cross-check (notebook Section 7)

### Summary
WarpFactory, the published warp-drive toolkit (Helmerich et al. 2024, CQG 41 095009), was given only the metrics. It independently reproduced the notebook's results: the paper's own Alcubierre example, every total-energy figure, and every Eulerian stress-energy component, including the switching dual shell at 1 GHz. Agreement is at round-off (~1e-15), except the grid-based paper check (7e-5, from the 5 m grid). Its energy-condition maps show the Vorwaller Metric breaking all four conditions throughout the wall and nowhere else.

### What Was Done
- `warp/warpfactory_check.py`: WarpFactory receives only a metric function, ds² = −dt² + (dx − S dt)² + …, i.e. shift βˣ = −S.
  - The cosine wall and the dual shell are written afresh as custom metrics.
    - The cosine wall's piecewise definition is wrapped so WarpFactory's own hyper-dual `cos` supplies the in-wall derivatives.
    - The dual shell's centre x_s(t) uses the exact drive antiderivative.
  - The Alcubierre metric is WarpFactory's own.
  - Nothing from warp.curvature or warp.energy is used on the WarpFactory side.
  - Totals use Gauss–Legendre in (r, θ) with the axisymmetric volume element.
- **Results:**
  - **Paper Alcubierre case** (Sec. 4.1: v = 0.1c, R = 300 m, σ = 0.015/m), via WarpFactory's 4th-order finite-difference GridSolver on a 5 m grid:
    - Peak Eulerian energy density: WarpFactory −6.7748e35 J/m³, ours −6.7751e35, paper "≈ −6.8e35".
    - Largest difference anywhere on the grid: 7e-5 of the peak.
  - **Cosine-wall totals** (Δ = 1 m … 1000 m, R = 100 m, β = 1): match the closed form to 3e-15.
  - **Alcubierre with a 1 m wall:** 2e-13.
  - **Full Eulerian T_âb̂ vs `curvature.einstein_frame`:**
    - Moving cosine wall: 1e-15.
    - All four dual-shell variants at 1 GHz (`time_dependent.shift_derivatives`): ≤ 1e-15. This is the first fully independent confirmation of the Section 6 switching terms.
    - The switching terms are ~all of max|T| there, so the check is not dominated by the frozen part.
  - **Energy conditions** (WarpFactory's `get_energy_conditions`, 100 directions × 10 speeds), Δ = 188 m wall:
    - Null and strong are broken at 99.7% of wall points; weak and dominant at 100%. None outside.
    - The 0.3% are where the violation goes to zero: the wall edges and near the axis.
    - (Retracted after review: an earlier note compared WarpFactory's worst weak-condition score, −4.2e38, with the Eulerian ρ, −8.4e37 J/m³, as "boosted observers see more". That score is a diagnostic, not an observer's energy density; see CODEX8 below.)
- **Tests:** `tests/test_warpfactory_check.py`, `importorskip("warpfactory")`; 12 tests at first, 21 after the review fixes (152 in total).
- **Notebook:** new Section 7 (WarpFactory); the summary moved to Section 8. Figures `07_warpfactory_energy_conditions` (2×2 maps) and `07b_warpfactory_agreement` (mass vs Δ, WarpFactory dots on our curve).
- **summary.pdf:** page 1's intro now says WarpFactory independently reproduced the results to ~15 digits. Still 5 pages; the spec caps it at 3–5.
- **Notebook context:** cites Fuchs et al. 2024 (CQG 41 095013, arXiv 2405.02709). Their subluminal warp shell satisfies all energy conditions; worded as "faster than light, no one has yet found a design that avoids negative energy", not as a theorem.

## External reviews of Task 6: CODEX8 (code), AGY3 (physics)

### CODEX8: no P1, five P2s, one P3. All fixed
- **Energy-condition minima aren't observer energies.**
  - The port normalises observer vectors by Euclidean length, and its "timelike" field includes v = c. The weak minimum came from a null vector.
  - Fix: dropped the comparison with the Eulerian ρ. The maps are now labelled as diagnostic scores, in the docstring and the notebook.
- **"Broken at 100%" was a rounded sampled fraction.**
  - 44 of 15,460 wall pixels had no sampled null violation; all were caught by axial null vectors. Black pixels only meant "none found".
  - Fixes:
    - `energy_condition_maps` now also returns contractions along the six axial null directions, computed from WarpFactory's own Eulerian-frame tensor; these also witness the strong condition.
    - The notebook combines them, giving 100.0% for all four with none outside, and reports WarpFactory-alone figures (99.7%) to one decimal.
    - Black now reads as "no violation found".
  - The port's dominant test checks only that the flux isn't spacelike, not that it's future-directed. This is documented as a narrower test; its negative scores are still genuine violations.
- **"Every stress to 15 digits" overstated.**
  - The pinned HyperDual.tanh uses 1 − tanh², so deep in a shell's tail it returns exactly 0 (e.g. T00 = 0 vs −3.5e8 J/m³ at r = 110 m, as written, against a peak of ~1e40).
  - Wording now says "within 1 part in 10¹⁵ of the largest value", with a tail note in the docstring.
  - Scope: Section 7 now says it confirms the energy and stress results (Sections 2, 4, 6), not the QI or the ship motion.
  - Added an independent comparison of the dual shell's energy through a cycle (WarpFactory total vs `dual_shell.mass_vs_time`): 2e-13.
  - summary.pdf page 1 now says WarpFactory "independently reproduced the energy and stress calculations" (was "the results to about 15 digits").
- **Python max hides NaN; a constant x_s offset cancels in the test.**
  - Fixes:
    - Finiteness asserts in the tests and the notebook's worst_difference.
    - New test: x_s(0) = 0, and centre(ct) matches `dual_shell.bubble_centre` for all variants.
- **Paper-check grid had y = 0 as an edge row.**
  - This gave a spurious 4.8e31 J/m³ on the axis, and the paper assert only tested our formula.
  - Fixes:
    - The grid now extends 4 rows below the axis.
    - Tests assert ρ = 0 on the axis to 1e-3 of the peak, and that the grid's own minimum reaches the peak.
- **P3:**
  - Odd n put a point at r = 0; it is now rejected.
  - imshow extent used pixel centres as edges; it is now ±HALF.
  - Also: ω > 0 is required, and the cosine wall's f″ convention at the edges is documented.
