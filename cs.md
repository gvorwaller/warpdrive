## Who this is for
- **The reader**: an experienced software engineer, not a math person. Every result needs a plain-English explanation.
- **The author** of the warp-drive ideas being tested: self-taught in general relativity. Views results as **images and PDFs on a phone**, and may print them.

This is a learning project. Accuracy matters, and so does explanation.

---

## Core Principles
- **No assumptions.** Read the papers and source before coding. State uncertainty; ask rather than guess.
- **No quick fixes.** Find root causes, especially for numerical problems (saturation, under-resolution, cancellation).
- **Every number is computed.** No placeholder or made-up results, and no hand-tuned values to make a plot look right. If something can't be computed yet, say so plainly.
- **The author's equations are theirs.** Implement them **exactly as written** first. Where something looks off, show "what it does as written" next to "the alternative". Never silently fix it. Tone: encouraging and honest, the way a good professor would handle it.

---

## Write-up Rules
- Each notebook section opens with a 2–3 sentence plain-English explanation.
- **Every chart has a short plain-English caption**: what it shows and why it matters.
- Celebrate the ideas that hold up. The thick-wall instinct is the right lever, and choosing the frequency against the wall-crossing time is real physics reasoning.
- Fix unit slips quietly in captions (e.g. `v_s² = 100c²`, not `100c`).

---

## Physics & Numerics
- Python 3, numpy, scipy, matplotlib; sympy for derivatives. Physical constants come from `scipy.constants`, never typed in by hand.
- Compute in geometric units if convenient, but **report in SI** (m, s, kg; energy as kg-equivalent `E/c²`).
- Floating point: `tanh(σ·r)` saturates for σ·r ≳ 20 (use stable forms or analytic derivatives). 1 GHz oscillations need enough samples per period. Check convergence: refine the grid and show that the answer holds still.
- The simple `T⁰⁰` formula assumes a **steady** bubble. Any time-dependent design must either include the time-derivative terms (full Einstein tensor) or say clearly that the formula misses them (spec Task 5).
- Reusable math lives in an importable Python module, not only in notebook cells, so it can be tested.

## Tests
- `pytest` covers every shape function: f ≈ 1 at the center, f ≈ 0 far away, and the derivative peaks where expected.
- Energy integrals get a convergence test against a known case (e.g. Pfenning & Ford's thin-wall `∝ R²/Δ` scaling).

## Figures (phone-first)
- PNGs go in `figures/`. They must read well on a **phone, as images in a chat app**: large fonts (≥12 pt at the saved size), thick lines, few series per panel, high contrast, and no colour-only distinctions (also vary line style or markers).
- `summary.pdf`: 3–5 pages, one chart per page, each with its plain-English caption. This is the copy to share or print.
- Figures are generated by running the notebook. Never hand-edit them.

## Development Workflow
- Virtual env in `.venv/` (gitignored); dependencies pinned in `requirements.txt`.
- Always `cd` back to the repo root; use absolute paths.
- Before every commit: `pytest` passes and the notebook executes cleanly top to bottom. Only commit when explicitly asked.
